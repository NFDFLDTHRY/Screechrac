--1. CORE ONTOLOGY
1.1. Reality
Reality (R):
Objective, constraint-bearing environment. Exists independent of any observer.
Not directly accessible by any mind.

1.2. Interface
Interface (I):
Biological and perceptual system (senses, nervous system, body state).
Converts Reality → raw internal signals.

--2. ARCHITECTURE COMPONENTS
Every human has these components. Differences are in parameters, not presence.
2.1. Dual Input Channels

1. RIC – Raw Input Channel
Input: structured signals from Interface:
“X said Y”
“door closed”
“bank account at $23.14”
timing, sequence, spatial relations, cause/effect traces

Role: preserve external structure as faithfully as possible.

2. PFC – Pre-Filter Channel
Input: same signals, but routed through:
priors
templates
roles
threat models
cultural narratives
ideology, religion, fandoms, etc.

Role: apply pre-existing schemas and assign probable interpretations before full compilation.

> Invariant:
RIC and PFC run in parallel, not serially. Both feed the compiler.

--2.2. Compiler (C)
Definition: Mechanism that converts structure → story.
Inputs:
Structured RIC data
PFC-shaped interpretations

Outputs:
Updates to the Story Ledger (L)

--2.3. Story Ledger (L)
Definition: Internal event log / ledger.
Properties:
Contains concrete, abstract, imagined, and inferred events.
Supports:
addition (new events)
merging (grouping)
re-indexing (changing what belongs with what)
deletion / suppression (pruning)

Ledger is not narrative form.
It’s the underlying data the mind uses to later construct narrative.

--2.4. Meaning Engine (M)
Definition: Weight system + transition logic operating on the ledger.
Inputs:
Current Ledger (L)
Internal value/priorities

Outputs:
Weights: how important each event/cluster is
Transitions: what should change in response:
beliefs
priorities
roles
future expectations
allowed behaviors

> In your model: Meaning is directive.
It doesn’t just label; it influences what happens next.

---

2.5. Emotion Engine (E)
Definition: Meaning-in-flight.
The runtime state of the system while the Meaning Engine is applying transitions.
Not:
meaning itself
story
intent

Is:
the experienced internal turbulence or resonance during change.

Emotions vanish or settle when transitions complete or stall.

> Emotions are not transmissible.
Only structure is. Others generate their own E from their M + L.

--2.6. Identity Shaping Layer (ISL)
Definition: Compression/synchronization layer where Story + Meaning + repeated emotional
patterns integrate into Identity.
Inputs:
Ledger state
Meaning weights and transitions
Emotional patterns over time

Output:
Current identity snapshot
Updated rules/priors for future compilation

> Identity here is not aesthetics or self-descriptions.
It’s the internal synchronization that determines “what kind of agent am I in this world?”

--2.7. Behavior Output (B)
Definition: External, observable actions.
Inputs:
Identity snapshot (ISL)
Current Meaning transitions
Current emotional state
Active templates and constraints

Output examples:
words spoken or withheld
physical actions
silence / freeze
jokes, escalation, retreat, attack, fawn, etc.

> Behavior is the only directly observable part of the loop.

--2.8. Feedback
Behavior alters Reality / environment.
New events become new structure.
Loop restarts.

--3. STATE MACHINE FLOW
Universal loop any mind runs:
S0 – Intake
Interface receives signals from Reality.
RIC gets raw structured events.
PFC applies priors/templates to the same events.

S1 – Coexistence & Early Pruning
RIC and PFC both active.
Early filtering:
some sensory/noise dropped
some things tagged as “threat,” “irrelevant,” “opportunity,” etc.

S2 – Compilation (C)

C takes {RIC, PFC} → updates the Story Ledger (L):
add events
merge them
reclassify them

S3 – Meaning (M)
M evaluates L:
What matters here?
How much?
What must change?

Produces weights + intended transitions.

S4 – Emotion Runtime (E)
E is the felt “meaning in motion” state.
stable meaning → calm
unstable meaning → turbulence

S5 – Identity Update (ISL)
ISL integrates:
new ledger shape
meaning weights
patterns of emotion

Produces updated:
> “Who am I now, given this?”

S6 – Behavior (B)
Action chosen based on:
Identity
Meaning
Emotion
Active constraints/templates

S7 – Feedback
Behavior changes outer structure.
New conditions feed back into S0.

Loop repeats.

--4. PARAMETER SPACE (HOW INDIVIDUALS DIFFER)
Same machine; different settings. These are the knobs.
4.1. Template / Schema Library (PFC Content)
What schemas exist for:
love, conflict, success, failure, authority, self, others

How strong they are:
Do they override raw input?
Do they only suggest probabilities?

--4.2. Priority Stack
When there’s conflict, what is preserved first?
Common candidates:
coherence
comfort
belonging
control
safety
novelty
power
harmony
image
autonomy
truth
sovereignty
status

peace

Priority stack is the internal ordering of these.

--4.3. Pruning Strategy
Where does most data loss happen?
Perceptual pruning (S0):
not noticing
habituation
selective attention

Pre-filter pruning (S1):
PFC discards inputs that don’t fit templates
“That can’t be what’s happening, so I ignore it.”

Post-compile pruning (S3–S5):
revise or erase uncomfortable ledger entries
reframe memory
down-weight events until irrelevant

--4.4. Arbitration Between RIC and PFC

When raw structure conflicts with template interpretation:
RIC-dominant:
“I trust what actually happened even if it breaks my stories.”
PFC-dominant:
“I trust my narrative/template even if it breaks the evidence.”
Coherence-dominant (Compiler):
tries to integrate both.
Identity-dominant (ISL):
reality bends to preserve self-concept.

--4.5. Meaning Engine Style
How M operates, functionally:
Descriptive:
meaning = labels (“this is trauma”, “that’s toxic”)
Evaluative:
meaning = value judgments (“good/bad”, “success/failure”)
Directive:
meaning = decision logic (“therefore I must / must not do X”)

Most people live in descriptive/evaluative.
Directive meaning is where sovereignty and consistent agency come from.

--4.6. Emotion Handling Style
When E spins up:
Decision-driving:
emotion → “I must act” (Mode D)

Story-rewriting:
emotion → “I must change what this means / meant” (Mode C)
Re-weighting:
emotion → “I must change how important this is” (Mode B)
Ignorable:
emotion is just noise; behavior follows templates.

People are usually mixtures.

--4.7. Identity Mode (ISL Style)
How Identity is formed and used:
Coherent: One continuous “self” across contexts.
Fragmented: Multiple selves; context-dependent.
Scripted: Identity = role being played.
Aesthetic: Identity = how one appears / is perceived.
Sovereign: Identity = anchored in internal constraints and authored meaning.

--5. SIX-ACT STACK ON THIS STAGE
This is your reusable “play” that any mind runs when processing an event or interaction. Acts
are process layers, not content.
STAGE ASSUMPTIONS
Reality exists and constrains.
Only structure crosses between minds.

Meaning and story are local.
RIC + PFC run in parallel.
The full loop (C, L, M, E, ISL, B) is present.

--ACT I – Scene & Stakes (Intake & Framing)
Question:
> “What world am I in right now?”

RIC: captures what actually happened.
PFC: decides what kind of scene this is:
argument, test, rejection, game, lesson, betrayal, opportunity, etc.

Output: perceived stakes + perceived roles.

Diagnostic prompts:
“What kind of scene is this to you?”
“If this were a movie, what type of scene would this be?”

--ACT II – Story Update (Compilation)
Question:
> “What just got added to my story?”

Compiler updates the ledger:
new event
merged event
re-tagged event

PFC may distort or gate raw inputs.

Diagnostic prompts:
“If you told yourself tonight what happened here, how would you tell it?”
“What line gets added to your internal ledger from this?”

--ACT III – Meaning & Emotion (Valuation in Flight)
Question:
> “What does this mean for me?”

Meaning Engine computes:
what matters most
what changes, if anything

Emotion spins up as meaning is applied.

Diagnostic prompts:

“What matters most about this to you?”
“What part of this hits you hardest?”
“What would be the worst-case interpretation for you?”

--ACT IV – Identity Integration
Question:
> “What does this say about who I am?”

ISL integrates:
does this confirm or threaten current identity?
does it force an identity shift?

Identity rules update for the next loop.

Diagnostic prompts:
“If this kept happening, what kind of person would you think it proved you are?”
“What does this make you quietly say about yourself?”

--ACT V – Behavior Emission
Question:
> “What do I do now?”

Behavior chosen under:
identity constraints
meaning weights
templates
emotional state

Diagnostic prompts:
“Given everything you’re feeling and thinking, what feels like the only thing you can do?”
“What options do you feel are off the table?”

--ACT VI – World Model & Template Update
Question:
> “What did this teach me about the world?”

Behavior + consequence feed back:
did the world confirm expectations?
did it violate templates?

PFC is updated:
templates entrenched

templates weakened
templates split

Diagnostic prompts:
“What does this convince you people/the world are like?”
“What rule about life does this reinforce for you?”
